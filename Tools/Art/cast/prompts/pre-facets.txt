First load dogsref.png from the current working directory with your view_image tool and study it closely. It shows this game's finished dogs, and it is the style, light AND camera reference for this image: the people you draw must look like they come from the same low-poly 3D game as these dogs, made by the same artist and rendered by the same renderer. Match its camera: the same high three-quarter view looking DOWN at about 30 degrees from above the front-left, orthographic, no perspective distortion, as if from a first-floor balcony; as with the dogs, the top of each figure's head and the tops of the shoulders are visible and the feet stand on an imagined ground plane seen from above; never eye level and never a straight-on front view. Match its light (soft even daylight from the upper left: lit faces upper left, shaded faces lower right) and its finish exactly: every surface (skin, face, neck, arms, hands, hair, clothes and shoes alike) is built from visible flat-shaded triangular facets of the same size and density as the dogs' facets in dogsref.png, each facet one flat matte colour with a crisp edge to the next, as on a low-poly 3D model; no outlines, no texture, no gradients, no smooth or realistic skin, no skin shading, no pores, no realistic or photographic face, no gloss, no painterly brushwork, no cartoon big eyes. Do not edit dogsref.png and do not copy its dogs; generate a new image with your image generation tool, giving it dogsref.png as the reference.
