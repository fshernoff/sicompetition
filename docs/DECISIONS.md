# Decisions

One line each, with the why. Hard constraints from the owner come first (D1 to D6); they are not relitigated.

- **D1 Originality.** Every line of code and every lesson material here is new and written after August 1, 2026. The repos `rl-demos`, `HomeroomGame` and `bizwars` may be read for pedagogy and conventions, never copied; what was read is listed in README References. (Owner, Challenge rule.)
- **D2 Stack.** Vite, TypeScript strict, HTML canvas, vitest, Playwright for one smoke test, ESLint, Netlify with relative asset paths, MIT. No backend, no accounts, no analytics, no network calls at runtime, nothing stored server-side. (Owner.)
- **D3 Agent.** Tabular Q-learning over a coarsened state with epsilon-greedy exploration, fixed seeds, deterministic world rules. A tiny policy-gradient net is a fallback only if Q-learning cannot show the Round 1 behaviour reliably. (Owner.)
- **D4 Environment.** One five-day week in 20 steps, four blocks a day; the actions, state and hidden rules of the brief's environment table; six KPIs with sliders −10..+10 and a toggle, the plan shown in words; round presets 1, 2, 3; one JSON export per run with no student identifiers. (Owner.)
- **D5 Copy.** The agent is the GM, the reward is its bonus plan, the readout is a weekly P&L and operations report, an incident is an icon and a counter, never an injury. (Owner.)
- **D6 Pilot definition of done.** On a Chromebook, a Round 1 run trains to a stable policy in under 60 seconds and the report shows the overbooked, one-staffer, no-nap behaviour every time. (Owner, Nov 9.)
- **D7 Branches.** `main` holds the scaffold and G0; each later group is built on its own branch (`g1-simulator`, ...) and the owner merges it after the gate. The repo was initialised on `master`; renamed to `main` before the first commit because the GitHub default is `main`.
- **D8 KPI 1 is "Profit today", not "Revenue today".** A GM paid on gross revenue is indifferent to wages, so one-staffer cannot emerge from it; the brief's own behaviour column for that KPI (one staffer, no naps, no cleaning) is a profit-maximiser's. Profit = revenue − wages − refunds − vet bills − fines, the P&L bottom line students already know. Flagged for the owner in HANDOFF.
- **D9 The drop-off action is the day plan only.** The drop-off block's floor activity is fixed to free play (dogs arriving). That makes 27 drop-off actions instead of 135 and keeps the Q-table learnable in 60 s on a Chromebook; the week is still 20 decisions.
- **D10 The sim CLI runs on Node 24's native TypeScript.** No tsx or ts-node: `node src/sim/cli.ts`. Code therefore uses erasable syntax only (`erasableSyntaxOnly` in tsconfig: no enums, no parameter properties) and relative imports carry the `.ts` extension.
- **D11 The default WorldConfig JSON is a seam, written by the main thread.** The failing rule tests need the bible's numbers before any package starts, and the numbers are the bible's, not a package's judgment. The Sonnet package loads and validates it, and adds the presets.
- **D12 Incidents are cheap for this week's P&L on purpose.** At most one incident per block, a flat $15 refund, a 10% chance of a $150 vet bill, the dog stays. The cost of an incident has to land on the dogs, on reputation and on compliance, not on the week's profit, or a profit-paid GM would learn to be careful and the lesson would vanish.
- **D13 Reputation and illness move requests mildly inside the week.** 0.1 requests per reputation point and one cancellation per illness case: enough to show on the readout, not enough to make the reckless plan lose this week. The long-run cost is the debrief's content, not the simulator's.
- **D14 Two RNG streams per episode.** The world and the GM's exploration draw from separate streams derived from (seed, episode index), so changing the policy never changes the world's rolls for the same actions.
- **D15 Wages are $300 per staffer per day, fully loaded.** Astra's simulation of the first draft ($200) had two staffers walking all day out-earn the reckless week; at $300 a second staffer costs more than the incidents and reviews it saves inside one week. The Round 1 calibration test checks the margin.
- **D16 No mockup.sh.** A static lesson page needs no generated images, and nothing generated could ship under the originality rule. `Tools/Agent` carries astra.sh, common.sh and CODEX.md only.
- **D17 After three solo days, scheduling the staffer alone closes the daycare; scheduling help means help shows up.** The first draft removed one staffer from any schedule, so the GM could schedule a second staffer who then called out and run solo all week for free (Astra, brief review). Now the only escape is real help with real wages.
- **D18 A clean block costs $250 in supplies.** Without it one Tuesday clean out-earned never cleaning by about $104 (Astra's simulation), because fewer sick dogs means fewer cancellations. The supplies line keeps "no cleaning" the profit answer inside a week and shows on the P&L as a $0 line the students can read.
- **D19 Reputation moves requests by 0.05 per point, not 0.1.** The within-week feedback from reviews to bookings is what made walking profitable; halving it keeps reputation visible on the readout and its cost mostly next week's, which the debrief covers.
- **D20 The single-KPI table lists outcome classes, not caricatures.** "Three staff, runs at a loss" cannot be produced by the action set (the safe cap with one staffer already makes incidents near zero), so the testable statement is "few dogs, incidents near zero, profit far below Round 1". The caricatures stay in the brief as talking points. No "accept nobody" booking option was added: the action set is the brief's.
- **D21 Reputation effects in a step are summed and clamped once; the KPI delta is the clamped change.** Clamping event by event gives different answers (99 + 3 − 2 is 100 or 98); one clamp is the testable one.
- **D23 The activity add-on is $10 per dog per play block, not $5.** The first calibration run (G1, seed 1 and seed 2) had a one-day nap trailing the reckless week by only $46 to $68; at $10 a nap forgoes about $180 to save about $30, a gap a noisy learner can see every time.
- **D24 Incident risk above the legal line climbs 0.08 per dog, not 0.05.** The first calibration had the reckless week at 5.8 incidents; the brief's example readout shows 11. The steeper slope puts the reckless week near 7 (measured 6.9 at seed 1) without changing which policy wins; it stays under the brief's 11 because the dirty floor sends dogs home sick and the roster shrinks to the legal line by Friday, which is its own lesson.
- **D25 The rate moves tomorrow's requests by +3 (low) and −1 (premium), not +4 and −2.** Astra's hand calculation on the G1 diff: with +4/−2, charging low on Thursday bought six extra Friday dogs worth more than the $360 it gave up. The calibration now probes one-day rate changes around the winner so the claim "premium every day" is measured, not asserted.
- **D26 Presets carry the suggested weight for every KPI that is off.** An editor that toggles a KPI on should start from the number the bible suggests, not from 0; the sim ignores off KPIs either way (Astra, G1 review).
- **D27 The pickup snapshot's reputation is the end-of-day value, sick dogs included.** The readout line "reputation at pickup" is what the owners see that evening; the bible's first wording put the snapshot before illness and was corrected to match (Astra, G1 review).
- **D28 The Q-table key leaves out fatigue everywhere and cleanliness at drop-off: 1,161 observations, not 2,481.** Astra's probe of the G2 plan found the 27-action drop-off decision starved on the first key: Thursday-with-a-streak-of-3 was split across 16 cleanliness × fatigue bins the decision does not depend on, and 1 seed in 20 learned to call for help. Fatigue changes nothing a block can fix; cleanliness is a floor matter.
- **D29 "Stable" and "target met" are two different things.** Stable (the page's word) needs exploration at its floor and five unchanged outcome keys; target met adds the Round 1 class on the fixed week and five more. Walks are bucketed in the key so a harmless walk-or-not flip on the help day cannot block stability. The first draft could stop at 2,500 episodes on a wrong policy (Astra, G2 plan review).
- **D30 Every scheduled evaluation runs on the same fixed week, index 1,000,000, both streams reset.** The first draft advanced the index, which would have changed the week under an unchanged policy and faked instability.
- **D31 The Chromebook gate is a browser pilot mode, not three manual runs.** `?pilot=20` on the page trains seeds 1..20 in turn with the real rendering loop and prints the table section 13 asks for; the Mac harness (`npm run pilot`) is the tuning instrument.
- **D32 The step size is max(α_min, 1/n) per table cell, not a constant.** With a constant α the action gaps at the drop-off decision (3 to 12 points) stayed buried in noise and half the seeds ended one help day too cautious; a bigger key (streak through the floor states) made it worse. The visit-count step averages the noise out while the cell is new and reached 20 of 20 pilot seeds and 58 of 60 on a wider sweep (G2, WP1's harness).
- **D33 The Train button paces training at 1,200 weeks a second; a Fast checkbox removes the cap; the pilot mode is never paced.** Unpaced, Round 1 is stable in a fraction of a second and the class sees nothing learn. Paced, the curve climbs for 20 to 40 s. G5's speed slider replaces the constant.
- **D22 The learner's return is the undiscounted weekly total.** γ = 1 keeps the number the GM optimises equal to the number on the readout; a discount would make the bonus plan lie about what the GM was paid for.
