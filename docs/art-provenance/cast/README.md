# The cast: provenance (G5d WP4a, D55, D59)

The yard's dogs and crew: 46 sprites in `src/assets/art/cast/` (36 dogs, two crew members in five poses each), listed
with their foot anchors in `src/assets/art/cast.json`. Generated for this project with GPT image (the built-in
`image_gen` tool of the Codex CLI 0.162.0, model `gpt-6-astra`, reasoning effort low, logged in to the owner's ChatGPT
account), the dogs on 2026-10-28 and the crew on 2026-10-29 (remade in the dogs' faceted treatment, G5d review round
2, row 13: below), one call per strip through `Tools/Agent/mockup.sh`, then keyed, cut, recoloured, repaired where
recorded below, graded and encoded in code by the scripts in `Tools/Art/cast/` (Pillow only). Nothing here is bought,
traced or taken from another work. D59's method: the cast generated, the set and the GM modelled in code
(`docs/art-provenance/set.md`).

## The crew remake (G5d review round 2, row 13; five calls on Oct 29, the budget about ten)

Astra raised it in round 1 (finding 17) and again in round 2, and the main thread confirmed it at 2×: the first
crew rendered smooth and near-photoreal (skin, faces, hands), with a more frontal camera, beside the dogs' faceted
low-poly treatment, so enlarged they read as cutouts from another source. The cause was in the calls: the first crew
call was told to keep spike A's smooth woman exactly (`crewref.png`), and the second matched the first one's finish.
The remake drops both and takes the style from the shipped dogs themselves:

- **The style reference.** `Tools/Art/cast/dogsref.py` lays seven of the shipped dogs' keyed, cut figures (what their
  sprites are built from, before the recorded repairs, the scale and the grade) on one 1536 × 1024 sheet of Floor's
  steel page, each resized by its breed's scale over the golden's, so every dog stands at one scale. Each call loads
  it first (`prompts/pre-facets.txt`
  replaces `pre.txt` for the `-facets` subjects): the people built like those dogs, flat-shaded triangular facets of
  the dogs' size on skin, face, hands, hair, clothes and shoes; the dogs' camera (looking down about 30°, the top of the
  head and shoulders visible) and light (upper left). The first call asks for the woman at the dogs' scale on that
  sheet (about 2.2 × the standing golden), so her facets come back the dogs' size in pixels; every later call is
  locked to her strip, so the scale and facet size carry.
- **Two strips a person.** Five poses do not fit one 1536-wide row at the cast's resolution, so each person has two
  strips: leash, ball and mop; then the leash pose again, kneel and tired, locked to the first. The repeated leash
  figure is only the second strip's scale (`build_cast.py` matches its height to the first strip's leash figure; it
  ships nothing). It came back the same height to the pixel in both people's strips (806 and 806 px; 877 and 877 px).
- **Who they are, kept.** The woman: light skin, brown hair in a ponytail. The man: deep brown skin, short black hair,
  a short beard, a little taller (his first call loads her strip for the uniform, the finish and the scale, as a
  different person). Both in the teal polo, khaki trousers, black belt and white sneakers; adult proportions; a
  simple low-poly face (brows, nose, cheekbones, small dark eyes, a mouth), neither blank nor detailed.
- **The new poses** (the contract's `CrewPose` gains `kneel` and `tired`): kneel, down on one knee at a dog's height,
  one hand out low and open as if greeting a dog, the other on a thigh or knee; tired, standing with the shoulders
  down, the head bowed, one hand at the back of the neck and the other arm hanging: worn out, not hurt or sick. No dog
  is drawn in either.

## From strip to sprite (`Tools/Art/cast/run_all.sh` reruns all of it in about a minute and reproduces the shipped bytes)

1. **Key and cut** (`key_cut.py`, one run per strip). The flags: `--no-pink` for every breed but the corgi (pink
   inner ears); `--no-green` for the beagle, the collie, the corgi and the yellow Lab (the dogs whose green fringe or
   pink speck D58 round 1 found, or whose strip was remade then; the passed golden, black Lab and brown poodle keep
   their bytes); neither for the crew (skin, lips, a red leash, a teal shirt). The key colour measured from the
   border; the key mask (magenta strength, and the key darkened in a crevice); each pixel compared with the
   figure-only 9 × 9 median round it: key-mixed pixels (salmon on a golden) and bounce-lit ones (a lavender rim on
   white fur) found; small sealed gaps closed (a group of key-mixed pixels up to 64 px with no pure key, or a pin
   hole up to 12 px; real gaps such as the leash loops stay open); key-mixed pixels unmixed, bounce taken out; the
   3 px edge band matted against the figure colour that best explains each pixel; key haze faded; a narrowed
   despill; a wide sweep against a 20 px surface colour; with `--no-pink`, the last pink or salmon in a coat with
   none returned to the coat's hue, pink or lavender on white or black made neutral, and a pale pink or lavender

## The pocket repair (D58 round 3, Oct 29; the main thread)

Sonnet's round 3 found three small enclosed pockets the hole rule leaves, because each holds a fully transparent
core that the soft edge links to the background: golden-gold-moving and golden-red-moving by the brisket
feathering (about (136-138, 87-89)), lab-yellow-asleep where the far ear meets the cheek (about (151-153, 59-63)).
`build_cast.py`'s `POCKET_REPAIRS` fills them before the encode (10, 10 and 11 px: a pixel with alpha 128 or less
that cannot reach the edge through such pixels, and its non-opaque ring, coloured from the opaque neighbours); two
rebuilds give identical bytes. Only those three sprites changed; their new passes are in `qa.md`.
