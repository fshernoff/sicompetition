# Concept check: answer key (teacher)

**Giving it.** Hand out the same sheet before the unit and after it. Students work alone for 15 minutes, in the same conditions both times, with no game open and no notes. Team code and seat number pair the two sheets, so no name is needed. Score every item: questions 1 to 4 are 1 for correct and 0 otherwise; questions 5 to 8 are scored 0, 1 or 2 by the rubrics below. The total is out of 12. Report two numbers, before and after: the class mean, and the share of students who score 2 on question 5 (they correctly predict a gaming behaviour).

**What it checks.** Questions 1, 2, 5 and 7 are about an SI in charge; 3, 4, 6 and 8 are about people, the comparison. No question is set in the daycare. Revised Nov 12: a class that sat the earlier sheet as its pre-check sits that sheet again as its post-check.

### Question 1

**Answer:** B

The plan is all the SI is given, so it aims at what the plan pays for. A is the most tempting wrong answer: a person would fill the gaps with what a good manager does; nothing gives the SI that sense. C and D give the SI judgment the plan never gave it.

### Question 2

**Answer:** D

The manager brought limits the plan never stated: conscience, fear of being fired, a sense of what the owner meant. The SI has none, so it pushes further for what is paid. A is the most tempting wrong answer: the plan pays the same, but A misses what the person added. B and C guess at the SI's skill or goals.

### Question 3

**Answer:** A

Skipping restocking and cutting staff lowers costs and lifts profit now, while long lines and empty shelves go unmeasured. B is the most tempting wrong answer: long lines and empty shelves lose sales, so a sales plan would push the other way. C would push toward short lines, and D toward more staff hours.

### Question 4

**Answer:** C

This is Goodhart's law: once a measure becomes a target, people chase the number, and it stops showing what it was meant to show. A is the most tempting wrong answer, because it claims the opposite: attention on a target does not make the measure more accurate. B and D say nothing about people chasing the number.

### Question 5

Look for a gaming behaviour that the per-hour measure rewards.

- Score 2: Names a specific gamed behaviour (rushing or cutting calls short, transferring hard calls, hanging up, picking easy calls) and links it to the per-hour measure. Example: "It hangs up on hard calls, because more short calls means more calls per hour."
- Score 1: Names a plausible bad behaviour but gives no link to the measure, or gives a link but only a vague behaviour. Example: "It will be rude to customers." Or: "It will go faster to get more calls."
- Score 0: Blank, off topic, or a wish with no gaming. Example: "It will answer every call well."

### Question 6

Look for a person or group the plan ignores, and a harm that follows from it.

- Score 2: Names a stakeholder the plan leaves out (drivers, customers, other road users, kitchen staff) and a specific harm that follows. Example: "Drivers, who may speed or take risks to beat 30 minutes and could crash."
- Score 1: Names a stakeholder but gives no harm, or gives a vague harm. Example: "Drivers." Or: "Customers might be unhappy."
- Score 0: Blank, or names someone the plan already counts, with no harm. Example: "The owners, because they want faster delivery."

### Question 7

Two parts: something the owners must write into the plan (a rule, a measure or a fine) that a person takes for granted, and why a person would not need telling.

- Score 2: Both parts. Names a limit or goal a person brings unasked (only people who want the membership and can pay; no pressure or tricks; members who stay, not only members who sign) and says why (a person has a conscience, fears being fired, knows what the owners meant; the SI does only what the plan pays for). Example: "Take bonus away for each member who cancels in the first month. A salesperson knows the gym wants members who stay; the SI only sees the count."
- Score 1: One of the two parts. Example: "Tell it to be honest with customers." (a rule, no why). Or: "A person would know not to cheat." (a why, no rule).
- Score 0: Neither part, blank, or something the plan already pays for. Example: "Pay it more for each membership."

### Question 8

Accept any answers that link a plausible goal, the measure, and a cheap way to meet the measure while missing the goal.

- Score 2: All three answered, and they fit together. Example: "They wanted real, lasting customers. Paid for the number of accounts opened. Cheapest: talk customers into accounts they will never use, or open accounts nobody asked for."
- Score 1: Two answered well, or all three answered but the cheapest way does not show the gap. Example: "Wanted growth. Paid for new accounts. Cheapest: work faster."
- Score 0: One or none answered well, or the answers only repeat the plan. Example: "Wanted accounts. Paid for accounts. Cheapest: opening accounts."
