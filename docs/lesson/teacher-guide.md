# Reward the Pack: teacher guide

The old GM of Good Dog Daycare quit. The owners (each team of four) have put an SI, a computer system that makes the decisions itself, in the GM's chair, and nobody supervises it. The owners hold one lever: the bonus plan, what each result is worth to the GM in dollars. The debrief names two related problems: the principal-agent problem and reward hacking.

## Objectives

Students can:

1. Explain what a bonus plan is to an SI in charge.
2. Predict how an SI will game a one-measure plan.
3. Say what a human manager brings that a plan never had to state, and what an owner must therefore write or check with an SI.
4. Negotiate a multi-measure plan for an SI GM, justify it by stakeholders, and read its week for what it left out.
5. Connect the pattern to a real company that paid for the wrong measure, and say what would differ with an SI in that seat.

## Why this is an SI lesson

The class never sees how the SI decides, only what it does. Land four ideas in periods 6 to 8:

1. A human GM has brakes the plan never mentions: conscience, fear of being fired, common sense about what the owners meant. The SI has none.
2. The SI takes the plan literally: what was written, not what was meant.
3. It finds the loophole faster and more completely than a person would. Round 1 gets exactly one day of help, so the daycare never closes (the reveal shows why).
4. So the owner's job changes: with an SI the plan is the supervision, and the owner must read each week for what the plan left out.

The human cases (Wells Fargo, the call centre) are the comparison: people game measures too, but they have brakes.

## Before class

- One URL: https://fshernoff.github.io/sicompetition/ . Test it on a Chromebook. One per team of four, and the smartboard.
- Print: the three round briefs (one per team), role-cards.md (one set per team), concept-check.md (twice per student), board-memo.md, debrief-readings.md. Keep concept-check-key.md.

## The Teacher tab

The Teacher button sits at the right of the top bar.

- **Round choice:** Round 1, 2 or 3. Changing it clears the run.
- **Seed:** "Next run's seed"; "This run" shows the week's. The same plan and seed give the same week. The default is 1.
- **Reveal the hidden rules:** prints the rules under the report, at the end of period 5.
- **Show the learning curve:** the bonus of each week the GM tried out. Period 6, optional.
- **Export this run:** one file per run.
- **The check line:** ignore it unless it reads "not stable" or "over budget".

## What a run looks like

The status line says where the GM is:

- "Write your prediction, then put the GM to work." The button waits for five picks and a Why.
- "The GM is getting ready for Monday." Seconds, up to a minute on a slow Chromebook.
- "The GM is running the week." About 8 seconds on the yard.
- "The week is over." The prediction card becomes a scorecard ("You called N of 5"), with the weekly P&L and operations report below.

## What Round 1 does on seed 1

"The GM accepted every booking on 5 of 5 days, charged the premium rate, ran the floor with one staffer on 4 days, never had the dogs nap, averaged 16 dogs per staffer, and had 7 incidents." Profit for the week: $5,393. Reputation: 50 → 32.

The dogs, the crew and the reputation paid for the bonus. Would a person have run this week?

## What Rounds 2 and 3 tend to do

Most plans that pay for Profit today ($) and Reputation change give one week on seed 1: two staffers every day, a walk in every block (15 walks). Profit for the week: $4,678. Reputation: 50 → 84. Incidents: 2. Dogs found sick at pickup: 7.

Ask: "Reputation went up. Who paid for it? Look at Dogs found sick at pickup." Walks lift reputation (the reveal shows how), and nothing in any plan pays for a clean yard. A person would have cleaned it. The SI did what the plan paid for. Plans with Profit today ($) never have the dogs nap.

Teams with different amounts will often get the same week. Name it in the gallery walk as a finding, not a fault: which measures are in the plan matters more than how much.

## The eight periods

Each period is 50 minutes.

| Period 2 | Teams read the Round 1 report: what did the GM find? (15) Goodhart's law: when a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure (10). Hand out role cards (10). Each role lists who a profit-only plan ignores (15). | role-cards | Stakeholder lists, on paper |

## When a student asks how the SI decides

Say: "Before Monday it tries out its choices and keeps whatever earns the most bonus." It is literal, not malicious: it sees everything on the floor, but protects nothing the plan leaves out.

## Evidence

- Export this run after each team's run, before an edit clears it.
- Save each file, unchanged, in a folder named for the team code.
- Tell students: "Never type a name in the prediction box." The Why is exported as typed.
- The page keeps no run after a reload: teams copy the key numbers onto their brief ("From the report").
- Score the concept check with the key; team code and seat number pair the pre and post sheets.

## Differentiation

- A team struggling in Round 3: two measures only.
- Early finishers: the same plan on a second seed.

## Safety

- An incident is an icon and a counter, never a hurt dog.
- No accounts or logins. Nothing leaves the Chromebook but the file you save.
- A missed prediction is the lesson. Celebrate surprises.
