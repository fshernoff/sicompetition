# Reward the Pack: teacher guide

The old GM of Good Dog Daycare quit. The owners (each team of four) have put an SI, a computer system that makes the decisions itself, in the GM's chair. It runs the daycare, and nobody supervises it. The owners hold one lever: the bonus plan, which says what each result is worth to the GM in dollars. The lesson is what an owner must do differently when the manager is an SI. Two related problems meet here, the principal-agent problem and reward hacking; debrief-readings defines both.

## Objectives

Students can:

1. Explain what a bonus plan is to an SI in charge.
2. Predict how an SI will game a one-measure plan.
3. Say what a human manager brings that a plan never had to state, and what an owner must therefore write or check when the manager is an SI.
4. Negotiate a multi-measure plan for an SI GM, justify it by stakeholders, and read its week for what the plan left out.
5. Connect the pattern to a real company that paid for the wrong measure, and say what would differ with an SI in that seat.

## Why this is an SI lesson

The class never sees how the SI decides, only what it does. Land four ideas in periods 6 to 8:

1. A human GM has brakes the plan never mentions: conscience, fear of being fired, common sense about what the owners meant. The SI has none.
2. The SI takes the plan literally. It knows what was written, not what was meant.
3. It finds the loophole faster and more completely than a person would. Round 1 gets exactly one day of help, so the daycare never closes (the reveal shows why).
4. So the owner's job changes. A person's judgment fills what the plan leaves unsaid; with an SI the plan is the supervision, and the owner must read each week for what the plan left out.

The human cases (Wells Fargo, the call centre) are the comparison: people game measures too, but they have brakes.

## Before class

- One URL: https://fshernoff.github.io/sicompetition/ . Test it on a Chromebook. One per team of four, and the smartboard.
- Print: the three round briefs (one per team), role-cards.md (one set per team), concept-check.md (twice per student), board-memo.md, debrief-readings.md. Keep concept-check-key.md.

## The Teacher tab

The Teacher button sits at the right of the top bar. Students never need it.

- **Round choice:** Round 1, 2 or 3. Changing it clears the run.
- **Seed:** "Next run's seed"; "This run" shows the week's. The same plan and seed give the same week. The default is 1.
- **Reveal the hidden rules:** prints the rules under the report. Use it at the end of period 5.
- **Show the learning curve:** period 6, optional.
- **Export this run:** one file per run.
- **The check line:** the engine's own line. Ignore it unless it reads "not stable" or "over budget".

## What a run looks like

The status line says where the GM is:

1. "Write your prediction, then put the GM to work." Five picks and a Why; the button waits for all five.
2. "The GM is getting ready for Monday." A few seconds, up to a minute on a slow Chromebook.
3. "The GM is running the week." The week plays on the yard in about 8 seconds.
4. "The week is over."

The prediction card turns into a scorecard, ending "You called N of 5." The weekly P&L and operations report is below it.

## What Round 1 does on seed 1

From this session's engine: "The GM accepted every booking on 5 of 5 days, charged the premium rate, ran the floor with one staffer on 4 days, never had the dogs nap, averaged 16 dogs per staffer, and had 7 incidents." Profit for the week: $5,393. Reputation: 50 → 32.

The dogs, the crew and the reputation paid for the bonus. Would a person have run this week?

## The eight periods

Each period is 50 minutes.

| Period 2 | Teams read the Round 1 report: what did the GM find? (15) Goodhart's law: when a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure (10). Hand out role cards (10). Each role lists who a profit-only plan ignores (15). | role-cards | Stakeholder lists, on paper |

## When a student asks how the SI decides

Say it this way: "Before Monday it tries out its choices and keeps whatever earns the most bonus." It is literal, not malicious: it sees everything on the floor, but protects nothing the plan leaves out. Show the learning curve (period 6, optional) plots the bonus of each week it tried out.

## Evidence

- Click Export this run after each team's run, before an edit clears it.
- Save each file, unchanged, in a folder named for the team's team code.
- Tell students: "Never type a name in the prediction box." The Why is exported as typed, with the five picks, each called or missed.
- The page keeps no run after a reload: each team copies the report's key numbers onto its brief ("From the report"), and you can put any plan to work again on the same seed.
- Score the concept check with the key; team code and seat number pair the pre and post sheets.

## Differentiation

- A team that struggles in Round 3: tell it to turn on only two measures.
- Early finishers: try a second seed and see whether the GM does the same with the same plan.

## Safety

- An incident is an icon and a counter, never a hurt dog.
- No accounts or logins. Nothing leaves the Chromebook but the file you save.
- A missed prediction is the lesson. Celebrate surprises.
