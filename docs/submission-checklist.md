## What gets submitted (verified Oct 28)

- **One PDF**: "no more than 10 pages PLUS a title page (11 pages total)", 12-point or larger, 30 MB at most. Sections
  in this order: Summary ("one page maximum"), The Approach, Safety, Learning Achievements, Next Steps, References and
  Acknowledgements. Only the Summary has a page cap; the bullets under each section are prompts, not required
  headings. **Title page** must carry: project title; team members *with the school* and the grades and/or subjects
  each educator teaches; the video link, "accessible with no password".
  - The Approach asks to "Describe all SI methods, tools, or platforms and how they will be used."
  - Safety asks how the team ensured "the physical and emotional safety of students and the security of any data used
    or created." (The district's SI guidance is not asked for here; the rubric's Safety line mentions "Guidance for
    safe classroom use", so it still belongs in the section.)
  - Next Steps asks what the team would add "if they were given three more weeks", and three prompts: what would change
    if the project advances to the regional competition; what it takes to make it "a fully functionable, deployable
    app or tool"; what work would "demonstrate full viability".
  - References asks for "all resources used to develop the team's solution, method, or tool, as well as any resources
    used to complete the project submission", plus thanks to anyone who helped.
- **One video**: one video, 4:00 at most (judges view only the first four minutes; credits, if any, count), 300 MB at
  most, shot horizontally. A link with no password, uploaded before the submission can be completed; test it "on
  several different types of computers". No format or host is named (YouTube is not mentioned). Someone off the team
  may shoot and edit; the subjects and content must be the team's.
- **The signed "Consent and Media Release for Educators, Team Leaders, and Volunteers"** (the form has three boxes:
  Team Leader, Educator, Volunteer; "check one"), uploaded as part of registration; wet or secure e-signature.
- **Code**: optional, and no rubric line scores it ("if seeing the code will strengthen your project, you can provide
  a link"). Ours still goes in, with the live game URL ("If you want the judges to be able to use your tool, you will
  need to have it available to them"): it is the evidence for SI Accuracy and Technical Validity (15).
- **Deadlines** (Eastern): **registration closes Dec 18, 2026, 5:00 p.m.**, and that one is hard: a late registration
  can register but "will NOT" be able to submit. **Submission closes Dec 23, 2026, 5:00 p.m.** Edits, or delete and
  start over, are allowed until then; editing the video or any part after the deadline disqualifies. The Team Leader
  submits through the portal. Our target for the submission stays Dec 18.
- **Eligibility**: 21+, full-time K-12 teacher, U.S. citizen, national or permanent resident; school email for all
  correspondence; "Proof of eligibility is required for educators at the state, regional, and national levels" (have
  it ready if we place).
- **Originality**: "100% of the project's content must be original content and created specifically for the Challenge
  after August 1, 2026." Nothing on adapting earlier work (open question below).
- **SI disclosure**: no tool is prohibited; not disclosing one is "academic dishonesty" and disqualifies; the rules
  also ask to "differentiate their own work from the work of others". Where to disclose is not said, so ours goes in
  both The Approach (the SI the students meet) and References (the SI that built it): Claude Code (build, lesson
  drafts, this write-up's drafts), the Codex CLI with GPT-6-Astra (reviews), GPT image (the illustrations,
  `docs/art-provenance/`).
- **Office hours**: weekly, Mondays, "approved registration is required" to use them. Remaining dates: Nov 2, 9, 16,
  23, 30; Dec 7, 14, 16, 21 (the rules say they end Dec 15; the timeline lists the 16th and 21st). Email
  SI.Challenge@doe.gov needs no registration.
- **After the state round**: state champions announced Feb 11 to 15, 2027 ($200 gift card each); regional is a live
  virtual presentation of "an updated project" with questions, Mar 4 to Apr 8, 2027 ($1,000 each and the DC trip;
  no regional rubric or length published); nationals Jun 6 to 9, 2027 in DC ($10,000 to "Educator Category team
  members", per person or per team not said). Every compliant entry gets a participation certificate by Aug 31, 2027.

**What changed from the Oct 25 plan:** the Dec 18 date is the hard registration deadline, not only our target; the
title page needs the school; the video link must be tested on several kinds of computer, not just a private window;
the code link is not scored by itself; Next Steps has three more prompts than we planned for; office hours need an
approved registration, so register before asking there.

**Fred's call (Oct 28): no student faces, no student names, the video shows the product.** No student appears or is
heard on camera, so no student media release is needed and the youth-release question is dropped. Student work
appears only as anonymous text or as a team's exported plans replayed on screen ("a student on team P2-C wrote ...").
